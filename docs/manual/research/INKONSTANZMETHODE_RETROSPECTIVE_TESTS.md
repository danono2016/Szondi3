# Inkonstanzmethode reconstruction — retrospective tests on Deri serial cases

**Status:** RESEARCH / FALSIFICATION WORK  
**Companion to:** `INKONSTANZMETHODE_HISTORICAL_RECONSTRUCTION.md`

This document puts the reconstruction hypotheses into practice on two complete serial records published by Susan Deri.

It does **not** claim to reproduce Böszörményi's method.

---

## 1. Why these two cases are useful

Deri provides two unusually valuable series:

- **F.T.**, Figure 5: ten profiles, described later as a severe compulsive/rigid case with high consistency, no vector mirror change, and not even one direct `+ ↔ −` factorial reversal;
- **B.I.**, Figure 19: nine completed profiles (the tenth box is blank), a severely confused schizophrenic patient deliberately presented as an example of extremely pathological change and erratic variability.

Deri explicitly contrasts them as structurally almost opposite cases.

That gives a falsification opportunity:

A candidate numerical instability descriptor should, at minimum, assign substantially more serial change to B.I. than to F.T. and should recognize the factors that Deri herself calls steady/root-like.

---

## 2. Transcription policy

The symbolic rows in Deri's published scoring sheets were transcribed from Figures 5 and 19.

For this first test:

- accent/loading marks are collapsed to the base reaction class;
- open reactions are represented as `0`;
- only the symbolic classes `{+, −, ±, 0}` are used;
- no attempt is made yet to reconstruct raw square counts from the upper graphic profiles.

This makes the test appropriate only for **H1c: simple consecutive sign-class changes**.

It is intentionally too coarse to test H1a/H1b fully.

## 5. First falsification result

### H1c survives as a coarse descriptor

The simplest sequential sign-change count correctly separates the two cases in the expected direction:

- F.T.: 17 changes / 9 intervals;
- B.I.: 33 changes / 8 intervals.

The distinction is especially striking because B.I. has **fewer** administrations yet almost twice as many symbolic transitions.

This gives independent retrospective support to the historical plausibility of a **successive-profile** change architecture.

It also makes Mélon's later IVG rule intelligible on older Deri material: a one-point-per-sign-change score is not arbitrary in relation to the serial phenomenology Deri describes.

### But H1c is probably too crude to be Böszörményi's exact method

The same test exposes its limitations.

For B.I., H1c correctly identifies:

- `m = 0` as perfectly constant; Deri explicitly says the m factor is minus throughout;
- `s = 1` as nearly constant; Deri says s is plus in eight of nine profiles;
- `k` and `p` as maximally unstable within this series.

For F.T., however, factors such as `m` receive several symbolic changes because they move between `+` and `±`, even though Deri emphasizes the **steady loading/ambivalent character** of e, k, and m and the global consistency of the series.

Thus a pure symbolic sign-change count can exaggerate changes that remain within a stable loaded/ambivalent organization.

This is exactly what Deri warned about when she said that the symbolized series is useful for rapid inspection but that the **intensity and type of change must be checked in the graphic profiles**.

## 6. Stronger implication for the Böszörményi reconstruction

The retrospective case test increases confidence in the following architecture:

**consecutive profile comparison is probably correct, but the elementary change score was likely richer than one point per symbolic class change.**

The most plausible family remains:

`factor reaction at t → factor reaction at t+1 → classify/quantify change → sum over intervals → factor Inkonstanzziffer`

A Böszörményi-like scoring function may therefore have combined some of:

- whether the basic direction changed;
- whether the reaction entered/left ambivalence;
- whether it entered/left an open state;
- whether a full `+ ↔ −` reversal occurred;
- how many graphic squares/choices changed.

This remains a hypothesis until the 1953 article is admitted.

## 7. Effect on competing hypotheses

### H1 sequential family

**Strengthened.** Two historically published cases behave exactly as expected under a consecutive-transition analysis.

### H1c simple sign-change count

**Validated as a useful coarse descriptor, but weakened as the likely exact Böszörményi formula.**

It captures gross instability but loses quantitative loading information that Deri insists matters.

### H1b categorical + magnitude weighting

**Strengthened conceptually.** B.I.'s direct reversals and rapid changes among open/ambivalent/opposed reactions are qualitatively very different from F.T.'s mostly constrained transitions. A category-aware score would likely separate them even more sharply.

### H2 all-pairs dispersion

**Further downgraded.** The most diagnostically emphasized distinctions in Deri concern what happens **from one testing to the next**, including abrupt versus gradual reversal. An order-free dispersion measure would erase precisely this temporal information.

H2 is not logically falsified, but it now has substantially less historical support.

---

## 8. A newly discovered historical triangulation

We now have three distinct serial-variability traditions that all privilege **successive** administrations:

1. **Deri 1949** — qualitative graded transition classes, with magnitude checked in graphic squares;
2. **David & Rabinowitz 1951 SIS** — successive-profile instability, but picture-level and factor-independent;
3. **Mélon 1974 IVG** — one point per factorial sign change across successive profiles.

Böszörményi's 1939–1940 method predates all three and is factor-level. None proves his formula, but together they make a **sequential transition architecture** much more historically probable than an all-pairs or order-free dispersion architecture.

## 9. Next executable research step

The next useful falsification is **not** another symbolic computation. It is recovery of the raw graphic counts `(n_sym, n_anti)` for F.T. and B.I. from Deri's upper profile panels.

Then test:

### H1a

`C_t = |S_t − S_{t+1}| + |A_t − A_{t+1}|`

per factor, summed over intervals.

### Nonparametric H1b surrogate

Without inventing historical weights, record separately for every transition:

- magnitude in squares;
- class: within-direction / to-from ambivalence / to-from open / direct reversal.

This will show whether Deri's qualitative contrast can be reproduced **without assigning arbitrary weights**.

If this succeeds, we will have reconstructed the likely *data structure* of Böszörményi's procedure even if the missing 1953 article remains necessary for the exact numerical scoring weights.

---

## Bottom line after first real test

The historical reverse engineering is no longer purely speculative.

On two published Deri series deliberately contrasted as rigid/consistent versus severely erratic:

- a consecutive symbolic change score separates them strongly;
- factor-specific constancy aligns with Deri's descriptions;
- direct reversal counts separate them even more dramatically;
- the remaining mismatches point precisely toward the information Deri says must be restored: **graphic loading/magnitude**.

This is substantive evidence for the architecture **successive factor-to-factor transition + quantitative/qualitative change magnitude**, while still leaving the exact Böszörményi `Inkonstanzziffer` formula unresolved.
