## 1. New archival lead: Szondi's Hungarian methodological lectures from 1939/1943

Hungarian historical bibliographies confirm that Szondi circulated methodological lecture texts in Budapest **before** Böszörményi's 1953 posthumous article:

- 1939: **Szondi Lipót, _Előadások a kísérleti ösztöndiagnosztika köréből. I. rész. Elmélet és metodika_**, Budapest, duplicated/mimeographed.
- 1943: **Szondi Lipót, _Előadások a kísérleti ösztöndiagnosztika köréből. I. kötet. Módszertan és ösztöntan_**, Budapest, Diószegi sokszorosítás.

The Hungarian historical literature also confirms that **Böszörményi György** belonged to Szondi's Budapest research group / seminar circle in this exact period.

### Why this matters for Inkonstanzmethode

The existing project documentation dates development of Böszörményi's Inkonstanz method to **1939–1940 under Szondi's direction**. Therefore the 1939/1943 Hungarian methodological lecture corpus is temporally capable of containing:

- an early form of the method;
- a transition taxonomy preceding the 1953 publication;
- worked examples or terminology later attributed to Böszörményi;
- a rule for factorial fluctuations that was not reproduced in the mature German Lehrbuch.

This is **not proof that the method is present**. It is a high-value pre-1953 primary/near-primary source lead that had not been recorded in the existing Inkonstanz research files.

This gives a concrete Hungarian acquisition target, not merely a bibliographic mention.

## 3. Second independent holding — ELTE Bárczi historical collection

A March 2023 ELTE University Library heritage feature states explicitly that an early Szondi test set **and the manuscript _Módszertan és ösztöntan_ explaining its administration and theoretical basis** are held in the:

**ELTE Bárczi Gusztáv Gyógypedagógiai Kar, Kari Könyvtár és Gyógypedagógia-történeti Gyűjtemény**.

The collection itself is described by ELTE as **open for research**. Current public contact information identifies the collection/library at **1097 Budapest, Ecseri út 3**, with the historical collection linked administratively to the faculty library. ELTE also states that the collection is undergoing digital inventory/processing.

This is materially stronger than a single-copy lead because the same 1943 methodological manuscript is now independently located in a collection institutionally descended from the very Hungarian special-education milieu in which Szondi worked.

### Restricted-digital possibility

ELTE operates a local **Könyvtár Böngésző** for digitized copyright-protected works physically held in its libraries, accessible only from dedicated library computers; screenshots/downloads are prohibited. Public indexing does **not** currently prove that the Szondi manuscript itself is available through that viewer. Therefore this is only an acquisition possibility, not a claimed digital copy.

## 4. The 1943 manuscript is actively cited by modern Hungarian historians

Kőváry Zoltán's 2018 study of the intellectual development of Szondi cites **specific pages** from the 1943 manuscript, including `Szondi, 1943a, 225`, and distinguishes it from another 1943 unpublished volume (`1943b`).

This demonstrates that the manuscript is not merely a catalog ghost: modern Hungarian scholars have actually consulted and cited its internal pagination.

It also means a derivative citation-mining strategy is viable: search Hungarian theses/articles that cite `Szondi 1943a` with page numbers and inspect whether any quotations come from methodological sections dealing with serial reactions, factor changes, constancy, or quantitative interpretation.

No such derivative quotation resolving `± <-> 0` has yet been found.

## 5. Böszörményi identity key recovered from Hungarian records

A Semmelweis/Pázmány register of medical graduates lists:

**Böszörményi György Lipót**

among graduates of the Pázmány Péter University Faculty of Medicine (register index no. `4306`).

Separately, the Budapest telephone directory for September 1940 lists:

**Böszörményi György dr., orvos**, VI. Nagymező utca 51.

Given that the Szondi research-group history names **Böszörményi György**, and the later article is by **G. Böszörményi**, this supplies a new high-value search key: `Böszörményi György Lipót`.

### Epistemic caution

The currently recovered records do **not** explicitly say, in one source, that `Böszörményi György Lipót` is the author of the Inkonstanzmethode article. The identification is therefore **RECONSTRUCTED / very plausible**, not SOURCE-VERIFIED identity linkage.

The important operational gain is that Hungarian archival/library searches can now use the full three-part name rather than only `Böszörményi György` or `G. Böszörményi`.

## 6. Independent historical corroboration of provenance

The Hungarian Psychology History Museum's Szondi archive page lists the same pre-1953 methodological lecture series, including the 1939 and 1943 volumes.

A 1996 historical article in *Thalassa* about Szondi's Budapest laboratory lists **Böszörményi György** among the regular members/collaborators of Szondi's seminar circle beginning in 1938. The same paper cites the 1939 and 1943 duplicated lecture texts.

The paper also states that by 1939 Szondi was already running four training courses in experimental drive diagnostics, for university psychologists, physicians, special educators and psychoanalysts. This increases the historical plausibility that methodological innovations developed in the group could have circulated in lecture/manuscript form before formal publication.

This still does not tie Böszörményi explicitly to a page or section in those volumes.

## 11. Zürich Szondi archive — primary-period manuscript/correspondence route

A new source-acquisition route is stronger than another open-web sweep: the **personal Nachlass / Szondi-Archiv at the Stiftung Szondi-Institut in Zürich** demonstrably contains material from exactly the 1938–1940 Budapest period and a large body of chronologically arranged original manuscripts.

### 11.1 Direct evidence that 1939 Budapest-period material is in the Zürich archive

Karl Bürgi-Meyer's historical study cites two Szondi letters to Balázs Péter from 1939 as held at the **Archiv Szondi-Institut, Zürich**:

- 17 March 1939;
- Easter Sunday 1939.

The same historical literature cites the oldest known 1938 Szondi test profile from **Leopold Szondi's Nachlass, Szondi-Archiv, Stiftung Szondi-Institut, Zürich**.

Sources:
- https://www.epa.hu/05200/05246/00006/pdf/EPA05246_thalassa_1996_2.pdf
- https://szondi.ch/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/Szondiana2002.pdf

### 11.2 Original manuscripts were chronologically archived in more than 25 boxes

Ines Grämiger's 2006 account of the institute's Szondi room describes finding large piles of written material and a very large number of handwritten pages from Szondi's personal estate. She reports that archivist **Jenö Kutassy** separated the original manuscripts from the remaining material and filed them **chronologically in more than 25 protected archive boxes**. She also says that furniture rescued from Szondi's apartment contained **manuscripts and drafts of his books**.

Source: Ines Grämiger, `Leopold Szondi – Die Persönlichkeit in Bild und Handschrift`, *Szondiana* 2006, especially pp. 24–25:
https://szondi.ch/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/Szondiana2006_001.pdf

### What this demonstrates

**A — SOURCE-VERIFIED:** the Zürich institute preserves a substantial original-manuscript Nachlass, arranged chronologically, and material from the critical pre-war Budapest years demonstrably survives there.

### What it does NOT demonstrate

No recovered catalogue entry or online text yet names Böszörményi, `Inkonstanzmethode`, `qu`, `t`, `c`, or the missing `± <-> 0` rule inside those boxes.

### Effect on the edge case

**Neutral as evidence, very high as acquisition value.** If preparatory notes, lecture drafts, correspondence with Böszörményi, or editorial material for the posthumous 1953 article survive, they could yield EUREKA-A or EUREKA-C.

## 12. Zürich archive can be searched by proxy: documented precedent

Kőváry Zoltán's 2018 study contains an unusually important methodological footnote. He states that he could not travel to Zürich to inspect Szondi's estate, so his Swiss-Hungarian friend **Victor Varga**, following Kőváry's instructions and with the support of institute director **Alois Altenweger**, examined approximately **120 Szondi letters and the library** for him.

Source: Kőváry Zoltán, `Az egzisztencialista Szondi Lipót`, *Imágó Budapest* 7(1), 2018, footnote 9, p. 12 in the article PDF:
https://imagobudapest.hu/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/IB_2018_1sz_pp007-47_Kovary.pdf

### What this demonstrates

**A — SOURCE-VERIFIED:** targeted remote/proxy inspection of the Zürich Nachlass has actually been done for an external scholar. The archive is therefore not merely a theoretical onsite-only destination.

## 13. Bern Rorschach Archives — near-contemporary author-file route

A separate archival line attaches directly to Böszörményi's Swiss publication history. A 1940 paper is bibliographically recorded under:

**Georg Böszörményi und Franz Mérei, `Zum Problem von Konstitution und Prozess in der Schizophrenie auf Grund des Rorschach-Versuches`, Schweizer Archiv für Neurologie und Psychiatrie 45 (1940), pp. 276–295.**

The Rorschach Archives bibliography indexes the item with the holding key **`SA 1940 Boszormeny`**.

The University of Bern's **Rorschach Archives and Collection, Institute for the History of Medicine** is an active research archive and provides consultation/inquiry and reproduction pathways.

### What this demonstrates

**A — SOURCE-VERIFIED bibliographic/archival trail:** a `Georg Böszörményi` was publishing in Swiss psychiatric literature already in 1940, and the Bern Rorschach archival system has an author-keyed item for that paper.

### What this does NOT demonstrate

The Rorschach paper is not the Inkonstanzmethode, and its presence does not imply that Bern holds correspondence, raw protocols, or Szondi-method material. This route is therefore **author-file discovery**, not evidence for `qu` or `t`.

### Potential forensic value

If the author file/offprint carries affiliation, correspondence, biographical notes, or cross-references, it may help close the identity chain `Böszörményi György [Lipót]` = `Georg/G. Böszörményi` and may expose adjacent publications or archival references otherwise hidden by name variation.

## 14. 1947 first German Textband — chronological control, not yet evidence

The first German edition of Szondi's **_Experimentelle Triebdiagnostik_**, Textband, Huber 1947, is bibliographically and digitally attested. It sits chronologically between the reported development of Böszörményi's method (1939–1940) and the 1953 posthumous publication.

Current open indexing exposes the book and its general methodological contents but has **not** yielded an explicit Böszörményi/Inkonstanz passage in this pass.

### What this can test

After the 1943 manuscript is inspected, the 1947 Textband is a useful control for whether:

- a serial variability method was already incorporated into the first German synthesis;
- Hungarian terminology was translated or compressed;
- a method present in 1943 disappeared, changed, or entered later.

### What it cannot currently do

It does not presently discriminate `qu` from `t` and should not outrank direct archive/manuscript acquisition.
