## 1. Eyewitness: Molnár Imre

Molnár Imre (1909–1996) was a psychologist trained in Germany and an early participant in Szondi's Budapest laboratory circle. Later historical literature uses his recollections for the early training/testing period.

A 1996 history of the Budapest laboratory cites:

**Pázmándy Györgyi (1991), `Fórum. Beszélgetés Molnár Imrével`, _Magyar Pszichológiai Szemle_ 47(1), pp. 88–109.**

The same history uses Molnár as an eyewitness for the 1939 training courses and the early use of the Szondi test.

## 3. Stronger new source: Molnár's unpublished memoir manuscript

Kiss Enikő Csilla's 2014 historical study of Szondi cites an unpublished manuscript by Molnár under the exact title:

**Molnár Imre, `Ami a kulisszák mögött volt a magyar pszichológiában 1932–1976 között`. Kiadatlan kézirat.**

Kiss then cites the recollection as:

**`Molnár, i. m. 37.`**

This is important because it demonstrates that the manuscript has stable internal pagination and was actually consulted, not merely mentioned.

Kiss uses p. 37 for Molnár's recollection of Szondi's early training group. In that recollection, Molnár names the early trainees and says that the group initially resisted Szondi's theory and collected data intending to disprove it, after which the test/theory survived their empirical challenge.

### Epistemic status

**A — SOURCE-VERIFIED:** an unpublished Molnár memoir under this title existed, was consulted by a modern historian, and has internal page numbering sufficient for a citation to p. 37.

**C — CORROBORATED eyewitness proximity:** the quoted material places Molnár inside the early laboratory/testing group.

It does **not** establish any Inkonstanz classifier rule.

## 4. Bibliographic title/year variant recovered

Later Hungarian history-of-psychology scholarship cites what is very likely the same memoir in a slightly different bibliographic form:

**Molnár Imre 1990, `Ami a kulisszák mögött volt a magyar pszichológiában a XX. század közepén`. Budapest, m.s.**

Pléh's English-language discussion of Karl Bühler's impact on Hungarian psychology explicitly refers to Molnár's **autobiography, `Molnár (1990)`**, and uses it for Molnár's own early biography and German training.

A 2019 history of psychology at the Budapest Faculty of Arts cites:

`Molnár I. (é. n.): Ami a kulisszák mögött volt a magyar pszichológiában a XX. század közepén. Kézirat.`

### Interpretation

The currently recovered evidence strongly suggests one manuscript circulating under at least two title conventions:

1. `... 1932–1976 között`;
2. `... a XX. század közepén`;

with at least one bibliography assigning **1990** and `Budapest, m.s.`.

This identity between title variants is **B — RECONSTRUCTED**, not yet SOURCE-VERIFIED by a catalogue record or title page.
