## 2. Why the 1947 provenance matters — and the new caution

The 1989 publication is explicitly presented as Noszlopi's 1947 seminar lectures, and later Hungarian historical scholarship cites it as `Noszlopi (1947)` even while giving the 1989 Akadémiai edition. That makes the **core text potentially very important as a pre-1953 witness**.

However, a new control now prevents a stronger claim: the 1989 volume contains a prefatory essay by **G. Donáth Blanka** (pp. 7–15) that discusses much later Hungarian Szondi usage, including Mérei's **two-profile technique** and explicitly references the Vademecum line from the 1960s/1970s. Therefore the 1989 physical volume is demonstrably **not wholly a frozen 1947 artifact**.

### Epistemic consequence

The previous wording "digitized, chronologically near, method-focused source object" must be refined:

- **A — SOURCE-VERIFIED:** the 1989 edition is explicitly based on / presents Noszlopi's 1947 seminar lectures;
- **A — SOURCE-VERIFIED:** the volume also contains at least one later editorial/prefatory layer (G. Donáth Blanka, 1989) discussing post-1947 Hungarian practice;
- **D — HYPOTHESIS until page inspection:** the operational core chapters (especially pp. 42–66 and tables p. 121+) may preserve 1947 text substantially unchanged.

Accordingly, if an Inkonstanz rule is found in the 1989 scan, it **cannot automatically be dated to 1947** until its textual layer is identified. It could still be EUREKA-C if the passage can be shown to belong to Noszlopi's preserved 1947 lecture text or if the edition explicitly attributes the rule to the early Szondi circle.

This is an important downgrading of chronology certainty, not a downgrading of acquisition value.

## 5. Important transmission contrast: Lukács 1985/1989

A detailed table of contents for **Lukács Dénes, _A Szondi-teszt_** (1985; unchanged 1989 edition) is publicly exposed. Its interpretation section explicitly lists:

- qualitative/dialectical interpretation;
- edge-middle interpretation;
- complement method / concordance analysis;
- quantitative methods: direction-tension quotient, symptom percentage, drive formula, drive class, latency series;
- proportional methods and indices.

The table of contents does **not** name an Inkonstanz method.

This is not proof that the book body contains no reference to Böszörményi. It is, however, a useful **transmission control**: the mainstream Hungarian university teaching lineage visible in Lukács's TOC foregrounds concordance, quantitative and proportional indicators rather than explicitly naming Inkonstanz.

The current Szondi Foundation course description (2026) shows the same visible teaching architecture: 10-profile technique, quantitative/qualitative/proportional indicators and concordance analysis, again without naming Inkonstanz.

Epistemic status: **C — CORROBORATED transmission pattern only**. It must not be used to infer the original classifier.

## 6. New near-contemporary control: Zemplén György, 1948

A full OCR-searchable article was recovered from **Vigilia 13/2 (February 1948)**:

**Zemplén György, `Sors-elemző lélektan`, pp. 67–74 of the issue.**

The article gives a compact contemporary account of the Szondi genotest. Its most relevant passage says, faithfully paraphrased, that the formulas obtained from **repeated administrations** (`Az ismételt felvételekkel nyert képletek`) offer major possibilities both for diagnosis of illness and for characterization of healthy persons.

The surrounding text then discusses interpretation of individual choice reactions and the test's clinical/characterological use, but **does not expose**:

- Böszörményi;
- Inkonstanz;
- a factorial fluctuation score;
- `qu/t/c` classes;
- a transition table among `+`, `−`, `±`, `0`.

### What this demonstrates

**A — SOURCE-VERIFIED:** repeated-profile interpretation was publicly described in Hungarian by 1948 as an important part of the test's diagnostic value.

### What it does NOT demonstrate

It does not show that a formal fluctuation classifier was already published or taught in that source. It therefore cannot decide `± <-> 0`.

### Forensic value

This is a useful chronological control: by 1948 Hungarian public exposition already emphasized **serial/repeated profiles**, but the recovered short exposition still presents that importance qualitatively rather than through an explicit instability calculus. This increases the plausibility that the Böszörményi procedure circulated in a more specialized teaching/research layer rather than in general public summaries.
