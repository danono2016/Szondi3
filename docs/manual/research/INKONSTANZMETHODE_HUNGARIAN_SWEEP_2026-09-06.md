## 2. User-supplied Hungarian periodical: Honti B. László 1980

### Source

Honti B. László, **"Kétprofilos vizsgálatok Szondi teszttel. II. A Szondi teszt analóg mutatói"**, *Magyar Pszichológiai Szemle*, vol. 37 (1980), printed pp. 481–491.

The article occurs in the user-supplied scan `MagyarPszichologiaiSzemle_37.pdf`. The annual contents also lists it at p. 481.

External metadata control: MATARKA identifies the preceding Part I in *Magyar Pszichológiai Szemle* 36/5 (1979), pp. 487–497, and Vargha's adjacent Szondi paper.

### Relevant exact Hungarian statements

Printed p. 483:

> `Ha nincs felgyűlt feszültség ... a feszültség „kijárt” ... vagy viselkedésbeli ("0"), vagy tudatbeli ("±") manifesztációt okozott.`

The same paragraph says Szondi considered the **temporal variability of the indicators** important: `időbeli változékonyságát (tíz profilos technika)`.

Honti then defines four analog indicators corresponding to the four reaction signs:

- `+` -> `P`, **pozitivitás**;
- `-` -> `N`, **negativitás**;
- `±` -> `M`, **megosztottság**;
- `0` -> `K`, **kiürültség**.

Printed p. 490, for two profiles, Honti's **mutációs ösztönképlet** uses the absolute change of his `D` value, ranks factors by that change, calls the most changing factors (`1.` and `2.` ranks) **mutációs tüneti tényezők**, and the most stable factors **mutációs gyökér faktorok**.

### What this demonstrates

**C — CORROBORATED:** a Hungarian Szondi-methodological tradition explicitly treats temporal factor variability and factor ranking as diagnostically meaningful.

**C — CORROBORATED:** `±` and `0` can both be framed as forms of tension having "gone out" (`kijárás`), but Honti does **not** collapse them. He assigns them separate constructs: `M = megosztottság` and `K = kiürültség`.

**C — CORROBORATED:** Hungarian source vocabulary worth searching includes:

- `időbeli változékonyság`
- `faktor-reakciók`
- `kijárás`
- `megosztottság`
- `kiürültség`
- `mutációs ösztönképlet`
- `legjobban változó faktorok`
- `legállandóbb faktorok`

### What it does NOT demonstrate

Honti does not cite Böszörményi in this article and does not reproduce the historical `qu / t / c` weighting system. His two-profile `D` method is a later, different construction.

The article therefore cannot be used as Böszörményi's classifier.

### Effect on `qu` vs `t`

**No direct update.** It weakens only one possible *argument* for `qu`: the idea that `±` and `0` should count as the same state merely because both are forms of `kijárás`. In this Hungarian source they remain dynamically distinct. That is not evidence that Böszörményi assigned their transition to `t`.

## 4. Noszlopi–Volosin 1972: high-priority Hungarian transmission target

### Source discovered

Noszlopi László – Volosin Péter, **Szondi ösztöndiagnosztikai teszt (10 profilos technika)**, Pszichiátriai Rehabilitációs Intézet, Intapuszta, 1972, 207 pp., reportedly printed in 500 copies.

Bibliographic/contents sources:
- https://www.antikvarium.hu/konyv/noszlopi-volosin-szondi-osztondiagnosztikai-teszt-397313-0
- https://www.regikonyvek.hu/kiadas/szondi-osztondiagnosztikai-teszt-1972-pszichiatriai-rehabilitacios-intezet

### Why this source matters

It is a Hungarian manual specifically devoted to **10-profile Szondi technique**, published only 19 years after Böszörményi's article and within a Hungarian clinical training context. Its contents include test interpretation (`Az ösztönészlelő teszt értelmezése`, p. 69), drive diagnosis (p. 77), and tables (p. 179).

Vargha 1988 later cites it explicitly as a standard Hungarian reference for the 10-profile technique.

### What is currently demonstrated

**A — SOURCE-VERIFIED bibliographically:** the manual exists, its scope is directly relevant, and its contents make it a serious near-transmission candidate.

### What remains unavailable

No searchable full scan was recovered in this round. Web snippets do not reveal `inkonstancia`, Böszörményi, or transition classes.

### Effect on `qu` vs `t`

**Unknown.** This is now a priority acquisition/search target, not evidence for either hypothesis.

## 5. Vargha 1988 and 1994: later Hungarian psychometric transmission

### Source

Vargha András, **"A Szondi-teszt faktorainak reliabilitásvizsgálata"**, *Pszichológia* 8 (1988), pp. 255–278.

Open PDF:
https://real.mtak.hu/212099/1/263_Pszichologia_08.pdf

### Relevant findings

Vargha converts Szondi factor constellations into quantitative variables and explicitly separates:

- `NUL` for empty/null constellations;
- `AMB` for ambivalent/split constellations;
- `DIF = POZ - NEG`;
- `SUM = POZ + NEG`.

He reports very low test-retest reliability for `NUL` and `AMB` compared with `DIF`, and cites Hungarian manuals by Lukács (1985), Noszlopi–Volosin (1972), Mérei, etc.

A later 1994 paper again reports `megosztottság` and `kiürültség` as the least temporally stable indicators.

### What this demonstrates

**C — CORROBORATED:** later Hungarian psychometric work continues to treat null/emptiness and ambivalence/splitness as distinct variables with different quantitative definitions; temporal stability is explicitly investigated.

### What it does NOT demonstrate

No Böszörményi reference or `qu / t / c` classifier was found in the searchable 1988 paper. This is psychometric reliability work, not a reproduction of the Inkonstanzmethode.

### Effect on `qu` vs `t`

**Neutral.** It further cautions against semantic collapse of `0` and `±`, but gives no historical weight class.
