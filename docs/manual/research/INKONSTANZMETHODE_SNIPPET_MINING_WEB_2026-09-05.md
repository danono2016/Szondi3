## 2. Important later-formal clue: directed vs non-directed reaction families

A later French Szondi description available at szondi.fr states explicitly that reactions `±` and `0` are called **réactions symptomatiques**, opposed to `+` and `-`, called **réactions radicales**. The same source prints the 28 mature count distributions and treats `±/0` as one conceptual family versus `+/-` as the other.

This is NOT evidence that Böszörményi himself used the terms `symptomatisch/radikal` for his multipliers, and it must not be imported backward as a quotation. However, it independently preserves a formal two-family architecture in the later Szondi tradition:

- directed/radical: `{+, -}`
- non-directed/symptomatic: `{±, 0}`

That architecture is exactly the one implied by Janssen's compressed definition of `t-Veränderungen`: transitions from `+` or `-` to `±` or `0`, or vice versa.

### Consequence for the unresolved cell

This later evidence strengthens the **structural plausibility of `± <-> 0 = qu`**, because `±` and `0` remain inside the same non-directed family while `t` crosses between the two families.

It does **not** settle the question, because Janssen's immediately preceding presentation of Deri explicitly groups transitions to/from `0` — including `± <-> 0` — among the more substantial opening/closing changes. Thus the tension remains:

- Janssen/Böszörményi literal compressed classifier + later two-family Szondi formalism -> points toward `qu`;
- Deri's near-contemporary taxonomy, which Janssen says Böszörményi fits well -> points toward `t`.

## 3. Later variability literature located

The Szondiana bibliography confirms a methodological lineage after Böszörményi:

- Mario Berta & Hugo E. Silvera, `Indice de variabilité dans le test de Szondi: communication préliminaire`, *Szondiana III* (1962), pp. 142–146.
- later work on variability in *Szondiana VII* (1967).
- Jean Mélon, `La signification de l'indice de variabilité dans le test de Szondi`, *Szondiana X* (1974), pp. 145–147.

Mélon's later autobiographical account says he developed an `indice de désorganisation globale` and regarded it as more reliable than the Berta–Silvera variability index. These later indices are not Böszörményi's method, but their short articles remain high-value search targets for quotations or contrastive descriptions of the older method.
