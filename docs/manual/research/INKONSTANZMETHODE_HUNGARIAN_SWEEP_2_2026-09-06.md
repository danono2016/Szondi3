## 1. Exact Hungarian term recovered from the Hungarian Lehrbuch translation

The Hungarian edition of Szondi's *Lehrbuch der experimentellen Triebdiagnostik*, published as **Szondi Lipót, _A Szondi-teszt_** (Hatodik Síp–Új Mandátum, 2002; later Új Mandátum, 2007), gives the quantitative interpretation section as:

- `A mennyiségi értelmezési módszerek`
- `A kvantumszámítás módszere`
- **`Az inkonstancia-mutató módszere`** — p. 290
- `A linneus-módszer`

This supplies the actual Hungarian transmission term **`inkonstancia-mutató`**, which is a much stronger discovery key than speculative translations such as `következetlenség`.

### Epistemic effect

**SOURCE-VERIFIED as Hungarian terminology in the translated mature Lehrbuch.**

It is **not independent algorithmic evidence**: the Hungarian book is a translation/transmission of the mature German Lehrbuch, whose brief Böszörményi notice is already known. It therefore does not decide `± <-> 0`.

Operationally, future Hungarian searches should prioritize:

- `inkonstancia-mutató`
- `inkonstancia mutató`
- `inkonstancia-mutató módszere`
- `inkonstancia` + `Szondi-teszt`

Source record: Antikvarium table of contents for _A Szondi-teszt_, 2002/2007 editions.

## 2. 1939 laboratory report is publicly digitized and exactly contemporaneous with method development

A new concrete source object was located:

**Dr. Lőrincz Ákosné Sófalvy Rózsa, “Az állami gyógypedagógiai kórtani és gyógytani laboratórium munkássága,” _Magyar Gyógypedagógiai Tanárok Közlönye_ (1939), pp. 328–340.**

The 1939 annual table of contents confirms the article and page span. The individual issue record in the Hungarian National Digital Archive / ELTE Bárczi collection identifies:

- 1939, vol. 1, no. 9;
- publication date: 1939-11-01;
- creator includes Lőrincz Ákosné Sófalvy Rózsa;
- format: PDF;
- access: free / downloadable;
- ELTE handle: `10831/32728`;
- file name: `Magyar_Gyogyped_Tanarok_Kozlonye_1939_I_09.pdf`.

The current research interface could retrieve the catalogue/index metadata but could not reliably fetch the large underlying PDF for page-level inspection. Search-engine snippet mining also did not expose a Böszörményi or Inkonstanz passage from pp. 328–340.

### Why this source matters

The article is a report on the activity of **Szondi's own state special-education pathology and therapy laboratory in 1939**, the same year in which the existing historical record places intensive finalization and teaching of experimental drive diagnostics and the beginning of Böszörményi's 1939–1940 Inkonstanz work.

This makes it a near-primary institutional source that may contain:

- names attached to specific research tasks;
- descriptions of current test-development projects;
- serial-testing methods;
- terminology circulating before the 1953 publication.

### Epistemic status

**A — SOURCE-VERIFIED:** the article exists, its exact bibliographic location is recovered, and a public digitized issue exists.  
**D — HYPOTHESIS:** the article may mention Böszörményi's project or an early fluctuation method. It has not yet been inspected page by page.

Effect on `± <-> 0`: **neutral for now, but high-value source-acquisition route**.

## 3. Hungarian oral-history route: Molnár Imre

The 1996 historical study of Szondi's Budapest laboratory cites:

**Pázmándy Györgyi (1991), “Fórum. Beszélgetés Molnár Imrével,” _Magyar Pszichológiai Szemle_ 47(1), pp. 88–109.**

Molnár Imre was one of Szondi's earliest Budapest collaborators and was one of the leaders of the four experimental-drive-diagnostics training courses running in 1939. The 1996 historical article explicitly says its author also relied on repeated oral conversations with Molnár about early Szondi-test use.

Searches located bibliographic confirmation and later works quoting the interview, but not a publicly searchable full text of the 1991 interview in this pass. No snippet recovered `Böszörményi`, `inkonstancia`, or a transition taxonomy.

### Why this matters

Unlike later general histories, Molnár is an eyewitness to the laboratory at the exact development period. The interview is therefore a plausible route to internal terminology, division of labor, or unpublished methodological practices.

### Epistemic status

**A — SOURCE-VERIFIED:** the interview exists and is used by historians of the Budapest laboratory.  
**D — HYPOTHESIS:** it may contain recollections relevant to Böszörményi or serial test methodology.

Effect on `± <-> 0`: **neutral until full text inspection**.

## 4. Institutional proximity of Böszörményi reconfirmed, but no algorithmic promotion

Karl Bürgi-Meyer's historical article on the Budapest laboratory lists **Böszörményi György** among the known members of Szondi's research group in the fate-analytic period. The same source says that from 1938 Szondi held monthly seminars and that by 1939 four formal training courses in experimental drive diagnostics were running for psychologists, physicians, special educators, and psychoanalysts.

This corroborates that Böszörményi was not a distant later commentator but belonged to the laboratory/seminar environment in which the test method was being actively taught and refined.

However, this is **provenance evidence only**. It does not establish the missing classifier cell.

Status: **C — CORROBORATED** relative to methodological proximity; no effect on `qu` vs `t`.

These sources remain valuable because Hungarian teaching lineages sometimes preserve tabular rules omitted from general histories, but no claim can yet be made about their contents.
