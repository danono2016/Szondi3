## 2. A separate pre-1953 instability score: David & Rabinowitz 1951

A Hungarian critical Szondi document unexpectedly exposed the citation:

**Henry P. David & William Rabinowitz (1951), `The development of a Szondi instability score`, Journal of Consulting Psychology 15(4), 334–336. DOI: 10.1037/h0060485.**

PubMed independently confirms the exact bibliographic record (PMID 14861333).

This is chronologically important: it predates Böszörményi's posthumous 1953 publication by two years.

Later Hungarian psychometric work by **Vargha András** explicitly describes the David–Rabinowitz measure as an **Instability (Inst) scale measuring the difference between two administrations of the 48 Szondi pictures**. This confirms that the David–Rabinowitz score is a real repeated-test instability metric, not merely a bibliographic title.

### Critical caution

There is currently **no evidence that David–Rabinowitz's instability score is Böszörményi's Inkonstanzmethode**. The two procedures may be independent:

- David–Rabinowitz appears to compare changes at the level of the 48 picture choices between two administrations;
- Böszörményi's reconstructed method classifies **factor-reaction transitions** using `qu`, `t`, `c` and aggregates across factors.

Therefore David–Rabinowitz must be treated as a **parallel instability tradition**, not silently merged with Böszörményi.

However, because it was published in 1951 and concerns exactly repeated-test instability, it may be forensically useful in three ways:

1. it may cite or describe pre-publication European/Szondi fluctuation concepts;
2. contemporary reviews/dissertations may compare it with Szondi/Deri/Böszörményi fluctuation ideas;
3. its bibliography/citation chain may expose otherwise hidden pre-1953 instability literature.

## 3. Contemporary secondary witness to David–Rabinowitz

A historical Boston University dissertation/review recovered through search indexing states that stability in successive Szondi responses was considered an index of healthy functioning, notes excessive fluctuation in psychotic subjects, and says **David and Rabinowitz's instability scores** could be used to investigate variables influencing successive test profiles.

This supports the interpretation that their score belongs to a broader early-1950s research concern with serial instability, but still does not connect it to Böszörményi's `qu/t/c` classifier.

## 4. Hungarian technical spillover: Popper and repeated factor changes

A searchable full-text mirror of **Popper Péter, _A kriminális személyiségzavar kialakulása_ (1970)** contains concrete descriptions of factor reactions changing between first and repeated Szondi administrations, e.g. a `−k` reaction remaining unchanged in four cases and changing to `0` or `+` in others.

This is valuable as evidence that Hungarian clinical literature did explicitly track reaction-to-reaction changes between repeated profiles.

It does **not** expose Böszörményi's classification or any `qu/t/c` weighting in currently indexed text.

## 9. Continuation: the David–Rabinowitz Inst formula is now explicitly recovered from Vargha

A later Hungarian psychometric source by **Vargha András** gives the actual operational definition of the David–Rabinowitz `Inst` variable, not merely its name.

Faithful paraphrase of Vargha's definition:

> For each of the 48 Szondi portraits, calculate the **absolute difference between its sympathy-value in administration 1 and administration 2**, then sum those 48 absolute differences.

Formally, at the picture-choice level:

`Inst = Σ_{i=1..48} |r_i(1) - r_i(2)|`

where `r_i` is the sympathy/ranking value of portrait `i` in the respective administration.

### Epistemic status

**A — SOURCE-VERIFIED in Vargha's later Hungarian exposition** that this is how the `Inst` score attributed to David & Rabinowitz is formed.

It is **not yet SOURCE-VERIFIED directly from David & Rabinowitz 1951**, because the original three-page article body has not been recovered in this pass.

### Why this matters for our edge case

This sharply separates the American `Inst` score from Böszörményi's Inkonstanzmethode:

- `Inst` works at the **48 individual picture-values** level;
- Böszörményi works at the **8 factor-reaction transition** level and uses `qu / t / c` classes.

Therefore an `± <-> 0` transition has **no direct primitive status** in David–Rabinowitz's formula. The American score cannot by itself decide `qu` versus `t`.

This is an important negative discrimination: the 1951 paper is useful historically/citation-wise, but it is unlikely to provide EUREKA-B for the missing Böszörményi classifier unless it explicitly cites a European transition scheme.

## 10. Honti's `sorrendi technika` can now be characterized more precisely from Vargha

Vargha's later discussion explicitly describes the data generated by the **sorrendi technika** associated with the Honti line:

- each Szondi picture receives a **fine 8-level rating**;
- those fine ratings make it possible to reconstruct what conventional foreground and foreground-background choices would have been;
- conventional foreground scoring is only 3-level (positive / neutral / negative) at the picture level;
- foreground-background scoring is 4-level at the picture level;
- the 8-level order/ranking method is therefore a finer-grained **picture-choice measurement procedure**.

### Epistemic effect

This materially lowers the probability that Honti's `sorrendi technika` is itself a hidden reproduction of Böszörményi's `qu/t/c` factor-transition classifier. Honti may still cite or discuss Böszörményi, and the 1980 follow-up may still contain relevant analog indicators, but the core Honti technique is now seen to operate primarily at the **picture-ranking** level.

Thus Honti remains worth body inspection, especially for references and comparisons, but its priority as a likely direct EUREKA-C source is reduced relative to genuinely factor-transition-oriented sources.
