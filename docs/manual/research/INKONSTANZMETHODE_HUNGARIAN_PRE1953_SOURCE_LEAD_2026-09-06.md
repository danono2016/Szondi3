## 1. New archival lead: Szondi's Hungarian methodological lectures from 1939/1943

Hungarian historical bibliographies confirm that Szondi circulated methodological lecture texts in Budapest **before** Böszörményi's 1953 posthumous article:

- 1939: **Szondi Lipót, _Előadások a kísérleti ösztöndiagnosztika köréből. I. rész. Elmélet és metodika_**, Budapest, duplicated/mimeographed.
- 1943: **Szondi Lipót, _Előadások a kísérleti ösztöndiagnosztika köréből. I. kötet. Módszertan és ösztöntan_**, Budapest, Diószegi sokszorosítás.

The Hungarian historical literature also confirms that **Böszörményi György** belonged to Szondi's Budapest research group / seminar circle in this exact period.

### Why this matters for Inkonstanzmethode

The existing project documentation dates development of Böszörményi's Inkonstanz method to **1939–1940 under Szondi's direction**. Therefore the 1939/1943 Hungarian methodological lecture corpus is temporally capable of containing:

- an early form of the method;
- a transition taxonomy preceding the 1953 publication;
- worked examples or terminology later attributed to Böszörményi;
- a rule for factorial fluctuations that was not reproduced in the mature German Lehrbuch.

This is **not proof that the method is present**. It is a high-value pre-1953 primary/near-primary source lead that had not been recorded in the existing Inkonstanz research files.

Catalogue details:

- note: **Sokszorosított kézirat** (duplicated/mimeographed manuscript);
- subject terms: `ösztöntan`, `genoteszt`;
- shelf mark shown in the catalogue result: **Sz 90**.

This gives a concrete Hungarian acquisition target, not merely a bibliographic mention.

## 3. Independent historical corroboration of provenance

The Hungarian Psychology History Museum's Szondi archive page lists the same pre-1953 methodological lecture series, including the 1939 and 1943 volumes.

A 1996 historical article in *Thalassa* about Szondi's Budapest laboratory lists **Böszörményi György** among the regular members/collaborators of Szondi's seminar circle beginning in 1938. The same paper cites the 1939 and 1943 duplicated lecture texts.

This does not tie Böszörményi explicitly to a page or section in those volumes, but it closes the historical proximity gap: the potential source and the method's author belong to the same Budapest research setting and time window.
